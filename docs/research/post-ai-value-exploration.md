## Research Date: March 2026

## The Question

In a post-AI world where intelligence is a utility and everyone can afford the smartest mind on earth to build/do anything — what do people still want? What can't AI provide? What will people pay for?

This document explores two high-conviction directions and evaluates whether they warrant a new project separate from Artha.run.

# OPTION C: Taste-as-a-Service / Human Curation Marketplace

## The Core Thesis

When AI makes *execution* free, the bottleneck shifts to **deciding what's worth executing**. Taste — the ability to discern what's good, what resonates, what matters — is fundamentally human and becomes the most valuable skill in an AI-saturated world.

The Ira Glass "taste gap" concept is being reframed for the AI era: AI collapses the skill gap but does nothing for taste. If you have great taste but mediocre execution skills, AI is your perfect complement. If you have neither, AI just gives you faster mediocrity.

## Market Validation (Real Data)

### The demand signal is strong:

- **ShopMy** (curated commerce) hit **$1.5B valuation**, $70M raised, $1B+ annual platform sales — just for shopping recommendations by tastemakers
- **Human-generated content receives 5.44x more traffic** than AI-generated content
- **Consumer preference for AI content dropped to 26%**, down from 60% three years ago
- **"AI Slop" was named Word of the Year 2025** by Merriam-Webster — 500K deepfakes in 2023 → 8M in 2025
- **21-33% of YouTube's feed** may be AI slop or brainrot
- **74% of companies struggle to get value from AI** because raw AI output lacks brand voice, strategic direction, and quality assurance
- **The Browser** (one person reads 1,000 articles/day, picks 5) has 10,000+ paid subscribers at $5/month

### Thought leaders validating this thesis:

- **Gaby Goldberg** (TCG Crypto/Stanford): "Curators Are the New Creators" — consumers will pay for good taste
- **Foundation Capital**: "Capability is rapidly commoditizing. Dependability — judgment, timing, and trust — is not. That will be the moat."
- **NEXT Conference 2025**: "In a world where knowledge is cheap, curiosity, curation, and judgment become insanely valuable."
- **Robin Good**: Catalogued 22+ web services making revenue purely by organizing existing information

### What's critically missing:

1. **No horizontal taste marketplace** — ShopMy does shopping, Volumo does music, The Browser does articles. No platform works across domains (design, product, brand, lifestyle, business strategy)
2. **No "taste + AI execution" beyond marketing** — Averi, Motion, GetCrux all serve advertising. Nobody does this for product design, architecture, writing, business strategy
3. **No reputation infrastructure for curators** — No robust system to prove and build credibility across domains
4. **No anti-slop verification layer** — Etsy labels, YouTube detects, but nobody has built consumer-facing provenance/authenticity proof
5. **No platform bridges the Ira Glass gap** — "You have great taste but can't execute? Provide direction, AI does the rest." This exists in fragments but nobody has built around this thesis explicitly
6. **B2B "judgment-as-a-service" is wide open** — No startup has positioned itself as selling expert human judgment layered on AI output for enterprise decisions

## Product Design: How It Could Work

### Core Concept: A marketplace where human taste is the product and AI is the execution engine

#### For Curators:
1. Build a **Taste Profile** by making decisions across their domain:
   - "For a SaaS landing page, this layout > that layout"
   - "For a coffee brand, this aesthetic > that aesthetic"
   - "For a tech blog, this voice > that voice"
2. Curate by example — selecting, ranking, annotating real-world references
3. What they reject is as important as what they select
4. Domain-specific: great taste in fashion branding ≠ great taste in fintech UX

#### For Consumers:
1. Browse curators by domain, style, and track record
2. Apply a curator's taste to AI execution:
   - "Build me a landing page with @sarah's design taste"
   - "Write my About page in @james's editorial voice"
   - "Design my restaurant's brand identity using @maria's aesthetic"
3. AI generates, but filtered through the curator's taste model

#### Revenue Model:
- **Per-application fees**: Someone uses a curator's taste profile → curator earns
- **Subscriptions**: Follow a curator's evolving taste
- **Enterprise licensing**: Companies license a curator's taste as their "brand DNA"
- **Platform take**: 15-25% of curator earnings

### Who Are the Early Curators?

| Category | Why They Join | Example |
|----------|--------------|---------|
| **Designers** | Can't keep up with demand but have incredible taste | A brand designer with 50 waitlisted clients |
| **Creative directors** | Their job is literally "taste" but they're expensive to hire | Agency CD earning $200K+ wants passive income |
| **Influencers** | Already monetize taste through content, not directly | Fashion influencer with 500K followers |
| **Domain experts** | Know what actually works in their field | Doctor who knows which health app UX works |
| **"Undiscovered" tastemakers** | Great taste, no platform to monetize it | The friend everyone asks for design advice |

### What Makes This Defensible:

1. **Network effects**: More curators → more variety → more consumers → more curator earnings → more curators
2. **Taste is personal and multi-dimensional**: Unlike prompts, a taste profile is complex and can't be trivially copied
3. **Reputation compounds**: Track record (how many people loved outputs guided by their taste) builds over time
4. **Data moat**: Preference data (what curators choose AND reject) is incredibly valuable
5. **Cultural specificity**: Taste is culturally embedded — a Japanese design curator understands wabi-sabi in ways no AI can replicate

### Hard Problems to Solve:

1. **Representing taste computationally** — Not just prompts. Multi-dimensional preferences across aesthetics, tone, structure, pacing, density. This is the core R&D challenge.
2. **Proving taste is good** — Need outcome metrics. Did the landing page convert? Did the brand resonate? Without this, it's just opinions.
3. **Discovery and matching** — How does a consumer find the right curator for their specific need?
4. **Taste evolution** — A curator's taste in 2026 isn't their taste in 2029. System must handle temporal evolution.
5. **Attribution** — When AI generates using a taste profile, how much credit goes to curator vs. AI vs. consumer's brief?

# OPTION D: AI-to-Atoms Bridge

## The Core Thesis

The digital world is increasingly solved by AI. But **80% of the economy is physical**: food, housing, healthcare, manufacturing, logistics, energy. The company that makes it as easy to orchestrate physical-world execution as it is to spin up a website will capture enormous value.

Software has eaten the digital world, but **the physical world still runs on phone calls, emails, manual quoting, and fragmented systems.** The pieces for a unified platform exist — nobody has assembled them.

## The Critical Gap

```
TODAY'S FLOW (painful, fragmented):

IDEA → [research manually] → [find designer on Fiverr]
     → [find manufacturer on Alibaba] → [email quotes for 3 weeks]
     → [negotiate pricing] → [wait for samples] → [quality check]
     → [find 3PL on ShipBob] → [build storefront on Shopify]
     → [handle customer support] → [manage returns manually]

     Each step: different platform, different login,
     different pricing model, weeks of delay, manual coordination

FUTURE FLOW (what Option D builds):

"I want to create a premium candle brand with
 minimalist packaging targeting millennials"
     ↓
[AI ORCHESTRATOR handles everything]
     ↓
PRODUCT IN CUSTOMERS' HANDS

     One platform, one interface, AI coordinates
     all vendors and handles exceptions
```

### The 6 gaps nobody has filled:

1. **No universal API layer for physical-world execution** — No "Stripe for atoms." Each vertical (manufacturing, shipping, warehousing, assembly) has its own fragmented APIs, legacy EDI protocols, and manual coordination. Nothing unifies them.

2. **No end-to-end "idea to delivered product" platform** — Despite all pieces existing (AI design tools, on-demand manufacturing, logistics APIs), nobody has stitched them into a seamless pipeline.

3. **No AI agents that coordinate across physical vendors** — No general-purpose agent can say "get this part CNC machined, anodized at a finishing house, then shipped to my assembler." Multi-step physical workflows across vendors remain manual.

4. **The physical readiness gap** — Only ~15% of companies have reached full AI industrialization. Legacy MES/SCADA/ERP systems create data silos.

5. **No standardized frameworks for physical AI implementation** — Every factory is a snowflake. No coherent system connects simulation, data, AI models, robotics, and governance.

6. **The pilot-to-scale chasm** — Over 40% of agentic AI projects may be cancelled by 2027 due to cost, complexity, or risk. Physical world failure costs are measured in damaged goods and safety incidents, not just software bugs.

### Vertical Prioritization:

**Tier 1 — Start Here (APIs/infrastructure exists):**
| Vertical | Why First | Key Integration Partners |
|----------|-----------|------------------------|
| Custom merchandise/products | Low MOQs, print-on-demand exists | Printful, Gooten, Xometry |
| Food/beverage brands | Co-packers are API-ready | Various co-packers, 3PLs |
| Print/publishing | Fully digital-to-physical pipeline | KDP, IngramSpark |
| Event production | High-value, recurring | Vendor coordination |

**Tier 2 — Next (infrastructure emerging):**
| Vertical | Why Next | Challenge |
|----------|----------|-----------|
| Hardware products | PCB/enclosure manufacturing APIs maturing | Higher MOQs, longer timelines |
| Home renovation | High willingness-to-pay | Contractor coordination |
| Healthcare coordination | Massive market | Regulatory complexity |

**Tier 3 — Future (needs more infrastructure):**
- Custom manufacturing at scale
- Autonomous logistics (drone/robot delivery)
- Biotech/materials

### What Makes This Defensible:

1. **Integration complexity is the moat** — Connecting 100+ physical-world vendors, handling edge cases, managing quality across suppliers. Extremely hard to replicate.
2. **Data flywheel** — Every order teaches the system which suppliers are reliable, which routes are fast, which manufacturers hit quality specs. Data compounds.
3. **Relationship network** — Physical vendors require real relationships, contracts, credit terms. Slow-build moat.
4. **Regulatory knowledge** — Each category (food, electronics, cosmetics) has different regulations. Institutional knowledge accumulates.
5. **Demand aggregation** — As platform grows, it can negotiate better MOQs and pricing with manufacturers. Volume = leverage.

### Hard Problems to Solve:

1. **Quality control at a distance** — How do you ensure a manufacturer produces what the AI designed? Need inspection networks, photo/video verification, quality APIs.
2. **Liability** — When an AI-orchestrated product fails, who's responsible? Platform? Manufacturer? User? Need clear legal framework.
3. **Minimum order quantities** — Physical manufacturing has MOQs. Single user wanting 50 units is uneconomical. Need demand aggregation or on-demand partners.
4. **Speed expectations** — Digital is instant. Physical takes weeks/months. Users accustomed to AI speed will be frustrated.
5. **Capital requirements** — Physical goods require inventory, materials, tooling. Who finances this? Platform-as-financier or lending API integration?
6. **Vendor reliability** — Unlike API calls, physical vendors miss deadlines, ship wrong quantities. Need redundancy and fallback systems.

# SHOULD THIS BE A SEPARATE PROJECT?

## Yes. Build separately from Artha.

### Reasons:

1. **Different architecture** — Artha is optimized for digital-only output (websites, emails, tweets). Physical-world coordination requires fundamentally different infrastructure (vendor APIs, quality control, logistics tracking, inventory management).

2. **Different customer** — Artha targets people who want a "company in a box" digitally. This targets people who want to create **physical things** and bring them to market.

3. **Different business model** — Artha is SaaS/credits ($49/mo). This would be transaction-based (5-15% of GMV flowing through platform). A single user launching a product line worth $50K in manufacturing = $2,500-7,500 in platform revenue.

4. **Different brand** — "AI builds your digital company" is different from "bring your ideas into the physical world with expert taste."

5. **Different timeline** — Artha can iterate weekly. Physical-world integrations take months to establish and test.

### What carries over from Artha:

- Multi-agent orchestration architecture
- AI provider integrations (OpenAI, Anthropic)
- Payment infrastructure (Stripe, Stripe Connect)
- The founding team's understanding of AI agent systems
- User research on "what do people want AI to build for them?"

# PHASED BUILD PLAN

## Phase 1: Taste Layer (Months 1-6)
**Goal: Prove people will pay for curated taste applied to AI outputs**

- Build curator onboarding and taste profile system
- Start with 2-3 domains: brand design, product aesthetics, content voice
- Let curators monetize immediately through AI-generated digital outputs
- This is Option C standalone, generating revenue and validating demand
- **Revenue target**: $5K-20K MRR from curator subscriptions + consumer usage

## Phase 2: First Atoms Vertical (Months 6-12)
**Goal: Prove AI can orchestrate one physical product category end-to-end**

- Pick ONE physical category (recommendation: custom packaging/merch OR food brands)
- Integrate with 5-10 manufacturing/fulfillment partners
- Connect taste layer to physical output — curated designs → manufactured products
- Handle the full pipeline: design → source → produce → fulfill → deliver
- **Revenue target**: $50K-200K GMV with 10-15% platform take

## Phase 3: Expand & Compound (Months 12-24)
**Goal: Multi-vertical with data flywheel**

- Add 2-3 more physical categories based on demand signals
- Build vendor rating/reliability system from Phase 2 data
- Add demand aggregation for better MOQ economics
- Launch enterprise tier for brands wanting rapid product line expansion
- **Revenue target**: $1M+ GMV/month

## Phase 4: Platform Effect (Months 24-36)
**Goal: Become the default "idea to physical product" platform**

- Open vendor API for manufacturers to self-onboard
- Enable curator-designed product lines (curators as product creators, not just advisors)
- International expansion (manufacturing in multiple countries)
- AI handles 95% of coordination autonomously, humans handle exceptions
- **Revenue target**: $10M+ GMV/month
